<MdxParagraph>

Battersea Power Station carried London's electricity for half a century before falling silent in 1983. Four decades of decay, failed schemes, and public imagination transformed the building into something beyond architecture—a monument to industrial memory and urban possibility. Its 2022 reopening as mixed-use destination posed a question most heritage projects avoid: how do you honour what a place meant while making room for what it becomes?

</MdxParagraph>

<MdxParagraph>

The Experience Room serves as interpretive threshold—a space where visitors encounter the building's story before dispersing into shops, residences, and public programming. Incomplete Infinity contributed to real-time interactive content and large-format exhibition materials as part of the broader creative team led by Squint/Opera and Lusion. The work needed to hold multiple tensions: reverence without nostalgia, transformation without erasure, architectural scale without overwhelming intimacy.

</MdxParagraph>

<MdxParagraph>

Heritage interpretation usually fails in predictable directions—either embalming the past or ignoring it entirely. The installation aimed for a third position: the power station's reinvention as continuation of its essential character. A building that once converted energy for a city now converts attention, gathering people into a space still defined by monumental ambition. The content shipped for the public opening, installed within the Experience Room where it greets visitors entering one of London's most improbable resurrections.

</MdxParagraph>
